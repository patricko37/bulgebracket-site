One number, measured against your dates

Readiness is a score from 0 to 100 for each unit and for the whole interview. It goes up when you answer correctly from memory and drifts down when you leave material unreviewed, the same way your actual recall does.

Put in the firms you are targeting and the app projects when you will cross the line at your current pace. If the forecast lands after the deadline, it tells you how many more minutes a day would close the gap. That is the entire motivational system.

How a lesson goes

Every lesson is retrieval first. You answer before you are taught, because that is how the material sticks and how the interview works.

  1. You answer from memory

    Three to five minutes of real interview questions. Multiple choice, short math, and ordering the statements. Nothing is dressed up as a game.

  2. A wrong answer gets a reason, not a penalty

    The explanation names the exact step you skipped, in the words an interviewer would use. Then you move on.

  3. You say it out loud

    Once a unit is solid, you record a 60 to 90 second "walk me through" answer and get it back graded line by line.

  4. You review what you are about to forget

    Spaced review brings each idea back just before it fades. Two minutes a day keeps a backlog from ever forming.

What we left out on purpose

Hearts, lives, and keys
You are preparing for an interview, not feeding a mascot. A wrong answer costs you nothing except the thirty seconds it takes to read why.
Unbounded points
A streak tells you that you showed up. The readiness score tells you whether it is working. Neither inflates forever, so neither becomes the point.
Machine-written questions
Interviewers can smell a generated question, and so can you. Nothing reaches you until a person has written or approved it, and it reads like a second-year analyst talking you through it.
A lifetime license
Recruiting is a season. You pay for the season, and when it is over we stop charging you.

Common questions

I have no finance background. Is that a problem?
No. Unit 1 starts with how a bank makes money and what an analyst actually does all day. The three statements are taught from zero in Unit 3. Nothing assumes you have taken accounting.
I am already a sophomore. Is it too late?
No, but the pace changes. Put in your deadlines and the app tells you how many lessons a day get you there. For most sophomores starting in the fall, that is two to three.
Does it cover the behavioral side?
Yes. Unit 1 is your story, why banking, and the fit questions, with spoken practice for each. Technicals are most of the course because they are most of the interview.
How long is a lesson, honestly?
Three to five minutes, plus a two-minute review most days. The app shows the time before you start and does not pad it.
Is there an Android version?
Not yet. BulgeBracket is iPhone only while it is in beta.
What does the free tier leave out?
Everything from enterprise value onward, and spoken grading. The free units are complete lessons, not samples.
